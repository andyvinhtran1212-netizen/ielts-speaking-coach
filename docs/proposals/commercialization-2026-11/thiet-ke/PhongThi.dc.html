Reading Passage 1

The Return of Hand-Made Goods

A  For most of the last century, shoppers in large cities expected every high street to look alike. The same goods at the same prices were taken as a sign that life was improving. In recent years a quieter movement has pushed in the other direction, as small workshops began selling pottery, furniture and clothing made by hand.

B  Several reasons explain the change. One is curiosity: people can now watch a knife being forged or a chair being jointed, and many feel a connection with the maker that a factory product does not offer. Another is concern about waste, since an object made with care is more likely to be repaired than thrown away.

C  The makers themselves face real limits. A potter working alone can finish only a few dozen pieces a week, and materials bought in small quantities cost more. Prices are therefore high, and the market remains small beside that of mass production.
